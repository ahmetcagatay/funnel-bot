SECTION 01 — MENTAL MODEL

The web-to-app funnel I see in my head

When I open a web-to-app funnel, I'm looking at 9 distinct stages — each with its own drop-off rate and its own optimisation lever. Most teams treat this as one funnel; in practice it's three funnels stitched together, and the joins are where most money is lost.

The three sub-funnels inside

Sub-funnel A — Click to landing (the ad → page join)

This is media-team territory but CRO-adjacent. The hand-off between ad creative and landing page is where most quality-score damage happens. If your TikTok ad promises "lose 5kg in 30 days" and the landing page leads with "Improve your wellness", the message-match score crashes and CPM rises across every campaign downstream. I'd own this hand-off jointly with the performance marketing team — same metric, two owners.

Sub-funnel B — Landing to paywall (the conversion engine)

The heart of the role. Quiz design, personalisation, plan reveal, paywall psychology. This is where 80% of testable improvements live and where most of my hands-on craft is concentrated. Every question in the quiz earns its place by either improving personalisation, building commitment, or qualifying the buyer — questions that don't do any of those are a tax on conversion.

Sub-funnel C — Paywall to retained subscriber (the LTV engine)

Where web-to-app gets distinctive. The web-bought subscriber retains differently than the in-app buyer — RevenueCat's 2026 data shows web paywall M1 retention at 84.5% vs in-app 48.2%, but the curves crossed by M8 (in-app 30% vs web 20%). That suggests web subscribers come in hot and cool faster — onboarding and product activation post-purchase are critical, and most teams under-invest here because the buyer is already "won".

The headline insight

The largest LTV-moving lever isn't paywall copy or layout — it's plan structure and trial duration. RevenueCat's data is clear: teams that test plan price points, plan count (1 vs 3 vs 5 options) and trial length (3 vs 7 vs 14 days) consistently outperform teams that A/B test button colour. Most teams have this backwards.

What's also true (less talked about)

  • App Store route still wins on raw conversion. Users coming through the App Store search → product page → install converted at 1.65% on average vs 1.13% for web paywall users. Web-to-app loses some volume but compensates with subscription quality.
  • Hard paywall is back. The 2022 freemium consensus has broken — teams seeing trial→paid lift of 4-5x by switching from "free with limits" to "trial then pay". Trade-off: top-of-funnel volume drops 20-30%.
  • iOS 17.4 ATT attribution changes mean less deterministic data from Meta/TikTok ad clicks. Probabilistic attribution + first-party tracking (UTM-tagged landing + on-device install referrer where available) is the new normal. Adjust/Branch/AppsFlyer still useful for the deferred deep-link layer.

SECTION 04 — PAYWALL CRAFT

What I know about paywall optimisation

Paywall is where I'd want to go deepest, fastest. The conversion mechanics, the trial structures, the test backlog — this is the function the role exists to scale.

Hard paywall vs freemium — when each wins

The trade-off is volume vs quality. Hard paywall (must subscribe or trial to use) loses 50-70% of top-of-funnel — but the people who clear it convert at 10.7% T35 vs 2.1% for freemium (RevenueCat 2026). For high-intent utilities (Easy Fax, Free Up) hard paywall almost always wins. For habit-formation apps (Planty?) freemium with limits and an upgrade nudge can win — the upgrade nudge fires when user is already attached.

The trial structures I'd test

Structure When it works Trade-off
3-day trial High urgency, instant value (utilities, AI solvers) Lower trial start volume; higher trial→paid
7-day trial Habit-formation, learning curve apps Industry standard — safe default but rarely optimal
14-day trial Tools with delayed perceived value (Baby Journal) Higher abandonment by day 5; needs nurture
Hard pay (no trial) Strong demand + transparent value (one-shot prints) Volume drops; LTV per converter is highest
Pay-now-or-pay-later split Higher-AOV apps with hesitant buyers Operational complexity; needs Adapty/Superwall infra

Test priorities I'd recommend in order

  1. Plan price points — annual price especially. Often the single most LTV-moving variable.
  2. Plan count — 1 plan (decisive) vs 3 plans (anchored) vs 5 plans (paradox of choice). Most teams default to 3 without testing.
  3. Trial length — 3 vs 7 vs 14 days. As above, defaults rarely optimal.
  4. Hard vs freemium — heavy lever; needs sample size and patience.
  5. Plan reveal pacing — when in the quiz does plan/price first appear? Too early and friction kills; too late and feels like a switch.
  6. Then — copy, headline, layout, button colour. These are the last 20%.
The tooling I'd reach for

Superwall for paywall A/B testing infrastructure (no-code editor + trigger logic, 30+ variants), RevenueCat for subscription management and Experiments for in-app paywalls, Adapty as a viable alternative if a particular feature is needed. For web funnels: Heyflow / custom build for the quiz layer, GA4 + Mixpanel for analytics, Hotjar / Microsoft Clarity for session replay, Klaviyo / Customer.io for lifecycle email and SMS recovery.

SECTION 06 — AI IN MY WORKFLOW

How I use AI in growth work

You called out AI tooling as a "good to have". For me it's how I work — not as a magic-wand replacement for thinking, but as a leverage layer on top of it. Three specific uses:

1. Research and competitor benchmarking

Within an hour of starting this brief I had structured insights on RevenueCat's 2026 benchmark report, the BetterMe / Cal AI quiz funnel patterns, the Adapty vs Superwall vs RevenueCat trade-off matrix, and 12 PlusMinusOne portfolio apps with their App Store ratings. AI shortens the research-to-insight time by an order of magnitude — but only if you ask the right questions.

2. First-draft creative + copy variants

For paywall A/B testing, generating 8-10 headline variants and screening them before going live is a 30-minute job with AI vs a half-day without. The kill criterion stays the same; the variant pipeline gets faster.

3. Building demos and prototypes fast

The two funnel demos in Section 05 went from concept to deployed in ~4 hours each because I work with Claude as a coding partner. For a growth role this matters: when you need to ship a landing page variant for a TikTok campaign tomorrow, the cost of "building it properly" drops to "shipping something testable today".

Where AI doesn't help

AI doesn't replace product instinct, customer interviews, or knowing which test to run. Those still require time on actual users, qualitative listening and judgement. AI multiplies a good growth instinct; it doesn't substitute for one.

SECTION 07 — IF HIRED

My first 30 / 60 / 90 days

Days 1–30 · Listen, instrument, ship one quick win

  • Funnel audit across top 3 apps by traffic. Open the live ads → landing → quiz → paywall journey for each. Heatmap, session replay sample, conversion-path drop-offs in GA4 + Mixpanel.
  • Sit with growth, performance marketing and product. What's the current backlog? What hypotheses have been parked? Who owns what metric?
  • One quick-win test live by day 30. Likely a paywall variant on the highest-traffic app. High confidence, low risk — to build trust and establish how I'd want to run experiments going forward.
  • Tracking sanity check. Are GA4, Mixpanel/Amplitude, Adjust, RevenueCat events firing where I'd expect them? In my experience, ~60% of accounts have tagging gaps that mute the report.

Days 31–60 · One structural play + one new funnel

  • Stand up A/B testing infrastructure if not already operational — Superwall or RevenueCat Experiments for in-app paywall variants, Convert / VWO / GA4-server for web funnel variants.
  • Build a new web-to-app quiz funnel for one app (my recommendation: Planty — the category fits best). Branch from current onboarding, route 50% of paid traffic to the new flow, measure trial start rate and T7 conversion.
  • Lifecycle email recovery — for quiz starters who don't complete, paywall reachers who don't subscribe, trial starters who don't convert. Each has a different message and a different timing.

Days 61–90 · Compound and document

  • Three concurrent live A/B tests across the portfolio. Statistical rigor enforced — no underpowered tests reported as wins.
  • First QBR-ready case study from the completed tests. Internal Insight Library entries for each.
  • Cross-portfolio pattern matching. What worked on Photo Print probably has a cousin that works on Print Photo. What works on Planty probably informs Pixy. The point of running a portfolio of apps is exactly this compounding.
