EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

solarVis at a glance — and what the data says

solarVis is a Turkish-built, internationally-positioned B2B SaaS platform that consolidates solar design, sales, and project management for solar installers and EPC firms. The product is well-architected, the customer base is enterprise-grade for a domestic market, and the timing aligns with a $1.2B → $2.5B global market opportunity. The principal growth gaps are not product gaps — they are commercial: pricing transparency, quantified social proof, and category positioning against well-capitalised incumbents.

Top-line takeaway

solarVis enjoys an asymmetric advantage in the Turkish + adjacent regional market — banking-grade enterprise references (Garanti BBVA, Türkiye İş Bankası, Akbank, Yapı Kredi), localised tariff and regulation modules, and TR + EN language support. The 12-month commercial question is whether the company will commit to a differentiated international position against Aurora Solar and OpenSolar, or remain a strong regional player. Both paths are viable; they require different go-to-market motions.

The five strategic findings of this brief

  1. Market timing is excellent. The solar design software TAM more than doubles by 2033 at 8.8% CAGR. Residential and commercial both grow; commercial growth is the steeper curve.
  2. Pricing strategy is the most consequential undefined variable. Aurora Solar charges $159-259/month; OpenSolar is free (monetised via referral/hardware revenue). solarVis's silence on pricing creates friction in both buyer evaluation and category positioning.
  3. The website undersells the platform. Zero quantified outcomes (no MW deployed, no users, no proposal-time-saved metric), generic FAQ, no comparison content. The product is stronger than the homepage suggests.
  4. AI Sales Agent is the most defensible differentiator against Aurora's residential dominance and OpenSolar's price disruption. It deserves higher visibility and category leadership content.
  5. The Turkish enterprise reference base is undervalued internationally. A customer base that includes four of the largest Turkish banks is, in market terms, a credibility moat. Single line: "Trusted by 4 of Turkey's largest banks" outperforms a generic logo carousel.

Market drivers (the tailwinds)

  • Global solar installation growth. IEA projects 600+ GW added annually by 2027. Each installation funnel needs design tools.
  • EU REPowerEU package. 750 GW of solar capacity target by 2030. Direct demand expansion for European installer tools.
  • Residential solar maturation. Falling hardware cost is shifting the bottleneck to sales and design cycle time — exactly where SaaS tools compress margin.
  • Battery + heat pump bundling. Single-purpose solar design tools lose relevance as systems become multi-component. Integrated platforms (solarVis, Aurora) win.
  • AI-driven design. Automated obstruction detection, panel placement, and proposal generation are emerging table-stakes (Aurora Pro's AI obstruction is a key paid upgrade).

Market headwinds (the risks)

  • OpenSolar's free model. Below-price disruption in residential SMB segment. Monetises via hardware/financing referrals, not SaaS subscriptions.
  • Aurora's enterprise consolidation. Aurora acquired HelioScope; the company is consolidating both residential (Aurora) and commercial (HelioScope) markets in the US.
  • Hardware vertical integration. Tesla, SunPower, and others bundle proprietary design tools with their hardware, reducing third-party software demand at top end.
  • Regional regulation fragmentation. Tariff, interconnection, and incentive databases must be maintained per country. High operational cost.
Strategic implication

The market is structurally favourable but increasingly bifurcated: free + hardware-referral (OpenSolar) at the bottom, premium + AI-heavy (Aurora Pro) at the top. Mid-market SaaS at $50-150/month is a viable but contested position. solarVis's natural niche may be "enterprise-grade design at SMB price points, with strong regional regulatory depth" — but this requires explicit articulation.

Two non-obvious findings

First: solarVis is the only platform on this list that ships a productised AI Sales Agent — Aurora, OpenSolar, and others have AI features for design (obstruction detection) but not for the sales-conversation layer. This is a category whitespace opportunity.

Two-axis positioning analysis: price point (Y) × geographic strength (X). This frames the strategic choice solarVis faces in the next 12-18 months.

The three strategic paths forward

Path A · Premium Vertical SaaS in EMEA

Position solarVis as "the European Aurora" — premium pricing ($99-199/mo), AI features as the headline (AI Sales Agent is the wedge), region-specific tariff databases as the moat. Compete on integration depth, not price.

Pros: highest LTV per customer; aligns with banking/enterprise reference base; defensible against OpenSolar's free model. Cons: direct competition with Aurora's international expansion; longer sales cycles.

Path B · SMB Volume Play (Freemium)

Match OpenSolar's free tier with limits (5 projects/month, basic features), monetise paid tiers ($49-149/mo) for higher volume and premium features. Build EMEA installer marketplace.

Pros: aggressive top-of-funnel; community building; appeals to SMB. Cons: cannibalises higher tiers; harder to monetise enterprise references.

Path C · Banking & Distributor White-Label Platform

Leverage the existing banking customer base (Garanti BBVA, İş Bankası, Akbank, Yapı Kredi) to white-label a financing-integrated solar tool. Banks offer "Get your solar plan + financing in one place"; solarVis powers the engine. Repeat with European banks.

Pros: uses an existing asymmetric advantage; high contract values; defensible. Cons: long bank sales cycles; lower-volume but higher-revenue customers.

The author's read

Path A and Path C are not mutually exclusive — they are the same go-to-market with two motions. Build the premium SaaS for installers (Path A) and the white-label platform for banks/distributors (Path C) as two co-existing revenue streams. Path B (freemium) introduces strategic conflict and competes head-on with OpenSolar's structural advantage of hardware-referral monetisation.

SECTION 06 — WEBSITE & CONVERSION AUDIT

solarvis.co — what works, what leaks

A homepage built on Next.js with clean architecture and considered visual design. The product surface is well-explained but the commercial layer (pricing transparency, quantified outcomes, comparison content) is structurally underbuilt. The page is competent — what it is missing is the conversion-engine layer that turns competent into compounding.

What's working

  • Hero clarity. "Built for solar professionals" + "Design, quote, and sell solar. All in one place." 5-second message-match is achieved.
  • Concrete hero visual. 3D mockup with sample system output (8.4 kWp, €14,200, 14.2% ROI) is more persuasive than abstract product screenshots.
  • Workflow visualisation. 6-step process (Lead → Design → Feasibility → Proposal → CRM → Installation) is a strong product-narrative device.
  • Solid technical foundation. Next.js + image optimisation + Vercel/standard CDN delivery. LCP likely strong.
  • Real case study links. Three named customer stories (Solino, Arçelik, Energy4All) is above average for a vertical SaaS at this stage.

Friction inventory (prioritised)

F1 — No pricing visibility above the fold or in the navigation
I 9C 9E 8

Issue: the homepage shows "Get Started" CTAs but no price anchor. Buyers comparing solarVis against Aurora ($159-259/mo) and OpenSolar (free) cannot place solarVis on the price-quality map. A pricing page exists but is not surfaced.

Recommended: add a "Starting at €X/month" tag in the hero, or move "Pricing" to the primary nav. Either decision (transparency or strategic opacity) should be explicit; current state is by-default opacity.

F2 — Zero quantified outcomes anywhere on the page
I 9C 8E 6

Issue: no MW deployed, no user count, no proposals generated, no average proposal-time reduction. Aurora cites "thousands of projects per week" and OpenSolar cites "14,000+ installers". A B2B buyer evaluating SaaS adoption looks for these figures within the first 30 seconds.

Recommended: within 2 weeks, surface 3-4 specific numbers — "Powering X MW of solar designs annually", "Adopted by 250+ installers", "Average proposal time reduced from 4 hours to 20 minutes". Data probably exists internally; it just isn't externalised.

F3 — Testimonials describe sentiment, not outcomes
I 7C 8E 8

Issue: 8 testimonials on the homepage. Most say "efficient", "easy", "saves time". None say "reduced our proposal-to-close cycle from 11 days to 4 days" or "doubled our conversion rate". B2B testimonials only convert when they include before/after numbers.

Recommended: reach out to top 5 customers and request quantified version of testimonial. Should be deliverable in 2-3 weeks.

F4 — Generic FAQ with no objection handling
I 5C 8E 9

Issue: the 8 FAQ items are surface-level. Missing the questions every buyer actually asks: "How does this compare to Aurora?", "Can I migrate from PVsyst?", "What's the onboarding time?", "Do you offer SSO / SCIM?", "GDPR/KVKK compliance?", "What CRM does it integrate with?".

Recommended: double the FAQ with objection-handling content. Source from sales team's recorded calls.

F5 — No comparison content (vs Aurora, OpenSolar, PVsyst)
I 8C 7E 5

Issue: evaluating buyers search "solarVis vs Aurora" or "OpenSolar alternative". solarVis does not own any of these queries. Aurora, by contrast, ranks for "HelioScope alternative" comparisons.

Recommended: produce 3 honest comparison pages (vs Aurora, vs OpenSolar, vs PVcase). Each frames the trade-offs without disparaging — "Aurora is the better choice if you are US-residential focused; solarVis is the better choice if you are EMEA + need integrated heat pump modeling". Strategic SEO play.

F6 — Free trial CTA without specifying trial mechanics
I 6C 8E 10

Issue: the primary CTA is "Get Started" but unclear what happens next. Trial duration? Card required? Feature limits? Trial conversion psychology requires the answer to "what am I committing to" be one click away.

Recommended: add a sub-line under the CTA: "14-day free trial · No credit card required · Full feature access". Removes adoption hesitation at zero cost.

F7 — AI Sales Agent under-marketed despite differentiation value
I 7C 7E 6

Issue: AI Sales Agent is the most defensible whitespace in solarVis's stack (no competitor ships an equivalent). It is shown as one of three product cards on the homepage with a "New" badge — but the page doesn't articulate why it matters, what installer pain it solves, or what early customers see in conversion lift.

Recommended: dedicated landing page with a 90-second demo, 2-3 early customer outcomes, and a competitive comparison framing ("Aurora has AI obstruction detection for design. solarVis has AI for the sales conversation"). Category leadership positioning.

F8 — No security / compliance trust signals
I 6C 7E 5

Issue: banks (Garanti BBVA, İş Bankası, Akbank, Yapı Kredi) are customers — they would only purchase from a vendor with serious security posture. But the homepage shows no SOC 2, ISO 27001, GDPR or KVKK badges. Implied compliance, not communicated.

Recommended: badge row in the footer + dedicated security/trust page covering audit certifications, data residency, encryption posture, GDPR + KVKK compliance.
